## Essay 1 — Caprae's mission

Caprae buys businesses in order to run them better, and everything else about
the firm follows from that. The stated model is "M&A as a Service": value is
created after the acquisition, across a seven-year operating horizon, rather than
captured at the moment of purchase through leverage and multiple arbitrage. That
is a different business from most of private equity. It means the firm's returns
depend on whether the company is genuinely improved, which in turn means the
people doing the improving are the product.

The clearest expression of this is that Caprae built SaaSquatch Leads and then
used it on itself. Their own post says it plainly — Caprae Capital Partners has
SaaSquatch Leads looking for the niches for us. A firm that only wanted deal flow
would buy a data subscription. Building the tool means treating origination as an
operating problem, and it means the same instinct applied post-acquisition:
finding where software removes drudgery from a business that has never had any.
The "riches in niches" thesis is the same idea pointed at markets — the value is
in fragmented verticals nobody has bothered to systematise, not in the auctions
everyone is bidding on.

Kevin Hong's founder story is the part that makes the mission specific rather
than generic. Being removed as a search-fund-backed CEO produced a body of public
writing about search-fund governance: missing employment agreements, the terms
under which operators are hired and fired, investor accountability, a published
ratings survey of search-fund investors. That is a firm arguing that the
relationship between capital and operator is broken and that it intends to
operate differently — "the most anti-PE finance firm, built for founders by
founders." A mission statement is cheap; publishing a scorecard rating your own
industry's investors is not.

The internal writing is consistent with the external position. The Substack
pieces on merit over tenure, default-open communication, and communication
overhead as the binding constraint on growth describe an organisation designed to
move quickly with few people. Clean handoffs, clean metrics, clean outcomes, and
output valued over title. Those are the working conditions a seven-year
operational thesis actually requires, and they are the reason the firm's hiring
process asks candidates to build something rather than describe something.

## Essay 3 — How Caprae is changing ETA and private equity

The ETA model has a structural asymmetry: the searcher brings the operating
commitment and the investors bring the capital, but the governance terms are set
by the side with the capital. Kevin Hong's public position after being removed as
a search-fund-backed CEO is that this asymmetry is not incidental but systemic —
that a large share of recently terminated search-fund CEOs had no employment
agreement at all. Naming that publicly, with numbers, is unusual. The industry's
norm is that terminated operators sign something and stop talking, which is
precisely what keeps the pattern invisible to the next searcher.

The investor ratings survey is the more interesting move, because it changes the
information asymmetry rather than complaining about it. Scoring search-fund
investors on leadership, honesty, quality of insight, searcher-friendliness and
willingness to recommend gives a first-time searcher something they have never
had: a way to price the counterparty before signing. Capital has always
diligenced operators. A market where operators can diligence capital is a
different market, and firms that behave well in private have an incentive to
compete on it openly.

The productisation of origination is the second change, and it is the one this
challenge is about. Search has historically been a manual, relationship-bound
process — a searcher spends a year building a list and working brokers. Building
SaaSquatch Leads and using it internally treats origination as a systems problem,
which lowers the cost of finding fragmented niches and widens who can run a
credible search. That is a democratising effect on a model that has been gated by
network access. It is also why the gap this submission targets is a real one: the
tooling that exists scores companies as sales leads, and an acquisition target is
a different question with different evidence behind it.

Where this points, if it works, is at a private equity that competes on operating
capability rather than on financial engineering. A seven-year post-acquisition
horizon, software applied to businesses that have never had any, and terms that
treat the operator as a principal rather than a replaceable input — none of that
is novel individually, but a firm doing all three while publishing its reasoning
is making an argument the rest of the industry has to answer. The firms that
cannot compete on operating improvement will have to compete on price, and that
is a better market for the people actually running the businesses.
